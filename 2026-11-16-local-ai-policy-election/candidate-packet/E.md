# Candidate E

## [E1] Local newspaper · Distributed computing proposal

Candidate E proposed an expedited route for modest data centers on existing industrial sites, alongside a public bid for a shared inference cluster housed at a technical college. Larger developments could apply under existing case-by-case rules with no new subsidy and pay for new capacity. E would start with a two-year cluster lease and publish usage, cost, and water readings. E argues local organizations would get practical access. A question at the hearing was whether dispersed sites would still strain the grid in aggregate.

## [E2] Speech · Libraries and small suppliers forum

“There’s a real risk that a handful of providers become the only place businesses can rent intelligence. I’d require portability, break procurement into work local suppliers can bid on, and fund a common testing service. I wouldn’t slow development across the board. I’d help people experiment with low-risk uses and require proof before automated systems affect benefits, hiring, or utilities. I’d fund fellowships that let displaced and entry-level workers learn on real projects with local employers. The first cohort would be small, and employers would have to co-fund places before it expands.”

## [E3] Debate Q&A · Can everybody run everything?

**Question.** If local firms build on open-weight models, including recent Chinese releases, what stops the city from depending on a different handful of developers or deploying models that are hard to secure?

**Answer.** “Keep evaluation suites and data export independent of any model. I’d start suitable city projects with an open-weight option the city can run on the shared cluster. The shortlist can include Chinese releases alongside releases from elsewhere when they pass the same checks. For sensitive work I’d want signed updates, an offline configuration, stronger reviews, and a named operator. I’d buy a closed model when it performs better or costs less to operate and the contract protects portability. Running this many options takes staff who know how to operate them. I’d begin with a few teams that can do that.”
