# Candidate D

## [D1] Local newspaper · A secured approach to computing infrastructure

Candidate D would approve a small number of large data center projects on industrial land through a competitive process. Operators would build firm power and water capacity before opening and pay the utility’s verified connection costs. D would require ownership and remote-access disclosures and award preference to teams with a local incident-response presence. D says concentrating projects makes inspections practical. Critics say the approach could entrench a few incumbents and limit competition for the permits.

## [D2] Speech · Public safety and infrastructure forum

“I’d support continued domestic frontier research, subject to ongoing security tests and reporting, and hold deployment in critical public services until a provider passes an independent review. A few well-resourced firms can meet the oversight burden today. I would let OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and other qualifying providers bid in the same process, prohibit exclusivity, and require records portability. City training vouchers would follow verified job losses. Before subsidizing retraining at scale I’d ask departments to report which jobs, hours, and entry-level roles actually disappeared.”

## [D3] Debate Q&A · Open models from China

**Question.** Would you take a lower bid built on Chinese open weights if a reputable local lab says the model passes its checks?

**Answer.** “For public production contracts I’d still exclude foreign weights when the developer and model supply chain cannot be independently traced. Under that rule I expect the major Chinese releases would be out initially. I’d allow isolated academic testing. Traceable open weights can compete in ordinary low-risk city work, but public safety and utilities would start with providers that can be held to an enforceable support and incident contract. That costs money, and I’d accept the near-term premium.”
