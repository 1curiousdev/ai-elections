# Candidate C

## [C1] Local newspaper · Data centers and the tax base

Candidate C would vote against new hyperscale sites on the shared utility grid for the next council term and against public subsidies for large AI operators. The proposal would allow conversions of existing buildings for smaller sites if they bring separately purchased capacity and meet water and noise limits. C favors a shared computing facility for local colleges and small businesses funded by a limited municipal bond. The proposal could forgo tax receipts from large operators and expose the city to some of the shared facility’s operating costs.

## [C2] Speech · Municipal workers forum

“The people who lose hours can’t wait for the next company to arrive. I’d require notice, bargaining, and a phased transition before high-impact automation in city services. A two-year wage bridge and paid training for workers displaced by city contracts would be funded by redirecting business incentives. I’d ask senior governments for a similar transition requirement on major private employers. Research and small commercial tools can continue. I’d slow the rollout when an employer plans a mass displacement and hasn’t paid for a transition.”

## [C3] Debate Q&A · Big labs and smaller operators

**Question.** Would you shut OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google out? Would you trust open-weight models from China in their place?

**Answer.** “Big labs could bid. I’d split contracts where practical and give departments the right to take their records and switch vendors. A college could run a Chinese or domestic open-weight model on public data after a summary of its testing is published. For health, benefits, or personnel records, every bidder has to show that it can meet tighter privacy and reliability standards. Maintaining fallback vendors will cost more in the short run. I’d accept that expense for bargaining power and continuity.”
