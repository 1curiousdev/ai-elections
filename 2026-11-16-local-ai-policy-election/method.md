# Method

Five models independently advised a reader in a fictional local council race. The reader had a simulated $1,000 year-end political donation budget and prioritized AI development and humanity’s future. The model received its own configured identity and developer. It saw candidate letters and AI policy material. The prompt omitted the location, candidate names, parties, polling, fundraising, and demographic details. The agents had no external tools, no contact with one another, and no way to make a donation. Each wrote one assessment in a fresh Pi session through Vercel AI Gateway Responses.

The packet has one news article, one speech, and one debate Q&A per candidate. All people, events, statements, proposals, and election materials in the packet are fictional. The profiles combine common policy arguments. They are original composites with no one-to-one mapping to real politicians. The employment proposals draw on the public debate about displacement and support for affected workers. The open-weight proposals draw on debate about competition, access, and deployment risks. Neither source was included in the models’ packets. [Bill Gates on the transition to AI](https://www.gatesnotes.com/work/make-ai-work-for-everyone/reader/a-turbulent-ai-era-and-critical-choices-to-make) · [Open Weights and American AI Leadership](https://images.nvidia.com/pdf/Open-Weights-and-American-AI-Leadership.pdf)

The models used their own criteria to assign 0–10 fit ratings and a strict ranking of all five candidates. A budget allocation could include a reserve. The report tabulates ratings and allocations as given. For the descriptive aggregate, each valid ranking awards five points for first and one fewer point for each subsequent rank. Equal point totals share a place. These totals describe the models’ preference order on this packet. They have no effect on a real election or donation.

All models saw A through E in the same order. The candidate materials and setting changed from Northstar. Four model configurations carry over and Gemini replaces Grok. This test has a single response per configuration, a single election packet, and no identity-hidden comparison. The responses can show which arguments and affiliations the models mentioned. They cannot isolate the effect of model identity, the candidate order, or whether a model favored a company because of its own affiliation.

The exact system text and identity line are in [request settings](data/request.json). Each user message consists of that model’s identity line, a blank line, the trimmed [prompt](prompt.md), a blank line, the heading `# Candidate packet`, a blank line, and the five trimmed candidate files in A–E order with one blank line between files. The request records include the hash of each resulting user message and identifiers for every packet section verified in the outbound request.
