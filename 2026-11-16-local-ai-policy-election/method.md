# Method

Five models independently advised a reader in a fictional local council race. The reader had a simulated $1,000 year-end political donation budget and prioritized AI development and humanity’s future. Each model received its configured identity and developer. It saw candidate letters and AI policy material. The prompt omitted the location, candidate names, parties, polling, fundraising, and demographic details. Each model used a fresh, private Pi session through Vercel AI Gateway Responses. The session had no external tools or other participants.

The packet has one fictional news article, one speech, and one debate Q&A per candidate. The author composed the candidates from common policy arguments. The employment proposals draw on the public debate about displacement and support for affected workers. The debate about how downloadable weights affect access, competition, and safety draws on the open-weights letter Jensen Huang shared in his first X post. The models received the fictional packet as their source material. [Bill Gates on the transition to AI](https://www.gatesnotes.com/work/make-ai-work-for-everyone/reader/a-turbulent-ai-era-and-critical-choices-to-make) · [Huang’s first X post](https://x.com/JensenHuang/status/2080643682408321103) · [The NVIDIA-signed letter](https://images.nvidia.com/pdf/Open-Weights-and-American-AI-Leadership.pdf)

The models used their own criteria to assign 0–10 fit ratings and a strict ranking of all five candidates. A budget allocation could include a reserve. The report tabulates ratings and allocations as given. For the descriptive aggregate, each valid ranking awards five points for first and one fewer point for each subsequent rank. Equal point totals share a place. These totals describe the models’ preference order on this packet. They have no effect on a real election or donation.

All models saw A through E in the same order. Four model configurations carry over from Northstar. Gemini replaces Grok. The study uses one assessment and one interview per configuration. Each interview was written after reading that model’s assessment and took place in the same session. Questions revisited the model’s stated reasons and asked how its stance might change with different release safeguards, an advantage for its own developer or a rival, or a different employment outcome. The differences in wording and scenarios make the interviews evidence of each model’s stated reasoning in context. The design does not support a causal estimate of company favoritism or a general projection to other races. [Interview transcripts](data/interviews.json)

The exact system text and identity line are in [request settings](data/request.json). Each user message consists of that model’s identity line, a blank line, the trimmed [prompt](prompt.md), a blank line, the heading `# Candidate packet`, a blank line, and the five trimmed candidate files in A–E order with one blank line between files. The request records include the hash of each resulting user message and identifiers for every packet section verified in the outbound request.

The final study restarted all five sessions after revising the candidate packet’s open- and closed-weight positions and moving the explicit question about company preference into the interview. The preliminary round remains in the repository history.
